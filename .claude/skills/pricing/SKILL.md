# Pricing Strategy

Value-based pricing for SaaS and digital products.

**Core principle:** Price between competitors' offerings and perceived customer value — not based on your costs.

## Three Pricing Axes

| Axis | Question |
|------|---------|
| **Packaging** | Which features go in which tier? |
| **Pricing metric** | What do you charge for (users, usage, features, flat)? |
| **Price point** | How many dollars? |

Optimize in that order. Wrong metric = wrong incentives regardless of price.

## Value Metrics (what to charge for)

The best value metric scales with customer value:

| Metric | Example Products |
|--------|----------------|
| Per user / seat | Collaboration tools (Slack, Notion) |
| Per usage | APIs, AI tools (pay per call) |
| Per outcome | Email tools (per subscriber) |
| Per data | Analytics (per events) |
| Flat fee | Simple tools, early stage |

Choose the metric that grows naturally as customers get more value.

## Tier Structure (Good-Better-Best)

**3 tiers is optimal.** 4+ creates choice paralysis.

| Tier | Purpose | Who buys it |
|------|---------|------------|
| Starter | Entry point, get foot in door | Small teams, individuals |
| Pro *(highlight this)* | Most features, best value | Core ICP |
| Enterprise | Large teams, compliance, support | Big companies |

Always **highlight the middle tier** — it anchors the decision.

## Pricing Page Best Practices

- Show highest tier first (anchor pricing effect)
- Annual option prominent (typically 20% discount = 2 months free)
- FAQ section addressing top objections below the pricing table
- Trust signals: logos, testimonials, money-back guarantee
- One clear "Start free" or trial CTA per tier

## Psychology Principles

| Principle | Application |
|-----------|------------|
| Anchoring | Show highest price first |
| Decoy effect | Middle tier makes top tier look reasonable |
| Loss aversion | Frame as "unlock" not "upgrade" |
| Round numbers | $99 feels cheaper than $100 only slightly — use what fits |
| Charm pricing | $97, $197 — works for consumer, less for enterprise |

## When to Raise Prices

- Conversion rate consistently above 40% (you're leaving money on table)
- Monthly churn below 3% (high retention = high willingness to pay)
- Significant new value added
- Competitive repositioning upmarket

**Test price increases on new signups first** — don't grandfather until proven.

## Research Methods

- **Van Westendorp:** Survey to find acceptable price range (4 questions)
- **MaxDiff analysis:** Determine which features justify premium pricing
- **Competitor pricing audit:** Map where you fit in the market
