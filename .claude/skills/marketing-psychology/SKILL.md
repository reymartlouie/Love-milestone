# Marketing Psychology & Mental Models

Apply proven psychological principles to marketing decisions.

**Foundation:** "People don't buy products — they 'hire' them to get a job done. Focus on the outcome customers want, not features."

## Quick Reference: Problem → Model

| Challenge | Apply |
|-----------|-------|
| Low conversions | Loss aversion, social proof, friction reduction |
| Price objections | Anchoring, decoy effect, value framing |
| Retention issues | Sunk cost, commitment/consistency, progress effect |
| Awareness/adoption | Social proof, authority bias, mimetic desire |
| Urgency needed | Scarcity, loss aversion, deadlines |
| Trust building | Authority bias, reciprocity, transparency |

## Category 1: Understanding Buyers

**Jobs to Be Done** — people hire products for specific jobs. Identify the job, not the customer type.

**Confirmation Bias** — buyers look for evidence that confirms their existing belief. Meet them where they are, then shift gently.

**Status Quo Bias** — people prefer the current state. Switching cost is real. Address it explicitly.

**Peak-End Rule** — people remember an experience by its most intense moment and how it ended. Design peak moments and great endings.

**Mimetic Desire** — people want what others want. Social proof works because of this.

## Category 2: Persuasion Principles

**Reciprocity** — give value first. Free tools, guides, audits create obligation to reciprocate.

**Commitment & Consistency** — small yesses lead to big yesses. Get micro-commitments early (email signup → webinar → trial → purchase).

**Social Proof** — number of users, logos, reviews, testimonials. Specific is more powerful than vague ("10,000 teams" > "thousands of teams").

**Authority Bias** — credentials, press mentions, expert endorsements increase trust.

**Scarcity** — limited availability increases perceived value. Only use when real — fake scarcity destroys trust.

**Framing Effects** — "95% success rate" vs "5% failure rate" — same fact, different impact. Frame around gains for optimistic outcomes, losses for risk-averse audiences.

**Loss Aversion** — losing $100 hurts ~2x more than gaining $100 feels good. "Don't lose customers to [competitor]" often outperforms "gain new customers."

## Category 3: Pricing Psychology

**Anchoring** — first number seen becomes the reference point. Show highest price first.

**Decoy Effect** — middle option makes adjacent option look like a deal. Use for tier pricing.

**Charm Pricing** — $99 vs $100. Moderate effect in consumer, less in enterprise.

**Pain of Paying** — subscriptions reduce pain vs. one-time purchases. Freemium reduces pain further.

## Category 4: Mental Models for Strategy

**First Principles** — break assumptions down to fundamentals before rebuilding

**Inversion** — instead of "how do we get more conversions?", ask "what would destroy our conversions?" and eliminate those

**Occam's Razor** — the simplest explanation is usually correct. The simple change is usually the right test.

**Circle of Competence** — double down on channels and tactics where you have demonstrated advantage
